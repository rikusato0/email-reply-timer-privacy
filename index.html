Privacy Policy for WLAI Timer

Published as the privacy policy for the Chrome Web Store extension “Email Reply Timer” (also referred to as WLAI Timer).
Effective Date: October 1, 2026 · Applies to extension version 2.10.2 · Developer: White Lotus AI

Overview

WLAI Timer is a Chrome extension for internal workplace productivity use at White Lotus Bookkeeping. It times how long staff spend writing Gmail replies and, when the user chooses, records that time against a task in the firm’s own White Lotus Tasks dashboard. It also provides a drawing (markup) layer that can be placed over a page while the dashboard is screen recording. When the user switches it on, it can also name the user’s ChatGPT chats by client (see “ChatGPT Chat Names” below).

The extension sends its data to one destination of its own: the firm’s dashboard, reachable at terminal.whitelotusbk.ai (and tasks.whitelotusbk.ai, the former address for the same dashboard, which is being retired). It contains no third-party API keys. The only other service it talks to is ChatGPT (chatgpt.com), and only while the user has ChatGPT chat names switched on: it then reads and renames that user’s own chats from the user’s ChatGPT tab. Nothing it reads in ChatGPT is sent to the dashboard or anywhere else.

The extension does not capture the screen, a window, a tab, system audio or the microphone. It holds no capture code and requests no capture permissions. Screen recording is performed by the White Lotus Tasks dashboard in its own browser tab; see “Screen Markup and Recording” below.

This Privacy Policy explains what information the extension may access, how that information is used, and the limits of our responsibility.

Information the Extension May Access

Depending on the user’s browser permissions, account configuration, and use of the extension, the extension may access or process limited information necessary to provide its functionality, including:

  • The fact that a user has started or stopped working on an email reply;
  • Time-tracking information, such as start time, stop time, duration, and related metadata;
  • The browser’s time zone, sent with requests to the dashboard, and the local date of the work, sent with a time entry, so time is recorded against the correct working day;
  • Information entered or selected by the user when submitting time, such as the client, list, task and note;
  • Client, list and task names retrieved from the White Lotus Tasks dashboard so the user can choose where to log time;
  • The user’s work email address and staff name, as returned by the dashboard when the browser is paired, so time is logged under the correct person;
  • Gmail compose contents, in two ways: a one-way hash of the subject, recipients and body text, used only to recognise the same draft again so the timer can resume (the hash cannot be reversed back into the email); and the text of the reply and its quoted thread, which is sent to the dashboard to produce a task summary (see “Email Summaries” below);
  • The address (URL) of the active tab, used only to decide whether the markup overlay can legitimately be placed on that page — browser and Web Store pages are skipped;
  • While markup mode is on and the optional all-sites permission has been granted: the ability to place the drawing overlay onto pages the user visits. The overlay draws on top of the page; the drawings exist only in that page and are never transmitted or saved anywhere;
  • While ChatGPT chat names is on: the firm’s client names and abbreviations, retrieved from the dashboard to build chat titles; the user’s ChatGPT chat list and the contents of their chats, read in the user’s own ChatGPT tab only to work out which client a chat is about; and the sign-in token ChatGPT’s own page uses, held in memory for those requests only (see “ChatGPT Chat Names” below).

The extension is not intended to collect email contents for independent use by the developer. However, because the extension operates in connection with email-related workflows, it may technically interact with pages or interfaces where email-related information is displayed, and email text may be transmitted to the firm’s own dashboard in order to provide the summary feature described below.

How Information Is Used

Information accessed by the extension is used only for the following purposes:

  • To operate the timer;
  • To allow users to review, confirm, and submit tracked time;
  • To send user-approved time entries to the White Lotus Tasks dashboard;
  • To generate a summary of the email being worked on, for inclusion in the task record;
  • To draw the markup overlay on the page the user is viewing, and to relay the overlay’s Pause and Stop buttons back to the dashboard that owns the recording;
  • To name the user’s ChatGPT chats by client, when the user switches this on;
  • To maintain, troubleshoot, secure, and improve the extension;
  • To comply with applicable legal, security, or platform requirements.

The extension does not sell user data.

Email Summaries

When a time entry is saved successfully, the extension also sends the text of the reply and the quoted thread it was written against to the firm’s own dashboard, which generates a short summary and attaches it to the task as a comment. This happens as part of saving time; it is not a separate opt-in step, and users should be aware that saving time against a task will normally attach a summary of that email to the task record.

The text sent is limited to the compose window’s visible body text, truncated to 6,000 characters. Nothing is sent when the reply is too short to summarise, and nothing is sent for a save that could not reach the server and was queued offline. The email text is not stored by the extension — it is held in memory only for the length of the request.

The summary is produced server-side using the firm’s existing AI provider account. The extension itself holds no AI credentials and does not contact any AI provider directly. Email text is used only to produce that summary. It is not used for advertising, sale of data, or unrelated profiling.

Screen Markup and Recording

Screen recording is started and stopped from the Record button in the White Lotus Tasks dashboard, and the recording is performed by that dashboard page. The browser shows its own picker there, so the user chooses precisely which screen, window or tab is shared, and the browser indicates recording while it is active.

This extension’s only role is the markup layer: a drawing overlay it can place on a different tab, which a web page cannot do for itself. The overlay draws locally in the page, keeps its strokes only in that page’s memory, and discards them when markup is closed. Nothing drawn is uploaded, stored or transmitted by the extension. The overlay’s Pause and Stop buttons send only a command back to the dashboard, and it displays the recording clock the dashboard reports.

Because the extension never touches the video stream, it neither sees nor uploads the resulting clip. How a clip is captured, retained, discarded or stored is handled entirely by the dashboard and the firm’s arrangements for it.

Placing the overlay on pages other than Gmail and the dashboard requires the optional all-sites permission described below, which the user grants explicitly and can withdraw at any time.

ChatGPT Chat Names

This feature is off until the user ticks “Name new ChatGPT chats by client” in the extension popup, which is also when Chrome asks the user to allow chatgpt.com, if it has not been allowed already. While it is on, a ChatGPT chat the user starts gets the client’s abbreviation (or, for a client without one, its name) in front of the title ChatGPT gave it, for example “BIA - Budget review”. Older chats are renamed only from the “Clean up old chats” panel, which shows every proposed name first and renames only the chats the user ticks. A cleanup can be undone.

To find the client, the extension reads the user’s chat list and chats in the user’s own ChatGPT tab, through the same requests ChatGPT’s own page makes, signed in as the user. It looks at the client named in the chat’s app calls (in practice, calls to the firm’s Dagny app) and at client names or abbreviations the user typed; ChatGPT’s written answers are not used. A chat with no client, or with more than one, is left as it is. What the extension sends as a result is the new title, to ChatGPT. No chat text, title or ChatGPT credential is sent to the dashboard, to any AI provider, or anywhere else, and the extension does not use ChatGPT data for any other purpose.

The sign-in token ChatGPT’s page uses is held in memory for those requests and is never stored or sent anywhere but chatgpt.com. Chats stay in the user’s ChatGPT account, subject to OpenAI’s own terms and the firm’s ChatGPT workspace settings; the extension changes only their titles. Unticking the box stops the feature and removes the extension’s script from chatgpt.com.

Browser Permissions the Extension Requests

  • Storage and alarms — to keep the pairing credential, cached task lists, local history and the retry queue, and to retry unsent saves on a timer;
  • Gmail (mail.google.com) — the timer runs only on Gmail; this is where the compose window is detected and the timer badge is shown;
  • White Lotus Terminal (terminal.whitelotusbk.ai, and its former address tasks.whitelotusbk.ai) — the dashboard the extension sends its data to, and the page permitted to pair the browser and to switch markup on. Both names are listed only so the extension keeps working across the address change; they are the same dashboard, and the old one is being retired;
  • Tabs and scripting — to place the markup overlay on the tab the user is looking at, and to check a tab’s address first so the overlay is not attempted on browser-internal pages; and, while ChatGPT chat names is on, to place the chat-naming script on chatgpt.com and open its cleanup panel there;
  • Optional: access to all sites — requested only when the user ticks “Let markup draw on any page” in the extension popup, and used only to place the drawing overlay. Declining it limits markup to Gmail and the dashboard. Unticking the box withdraws it immediately;
  • Optional: chatgpt.com — requested only when the user ticks “Name new ChatGPT chats by client”, and used only to read and rename that user’s own chats as described above. Unticking the box withdraws it, unless the all-sites markup permission, which also covers chatgpt.com, has been granted.

Authentication

Each browser is paired once with the dashboard, where the user signs in through the firm’s single sign-on. The browser then holds a personal access token stored locally in the browser’s extension storage. That token identifies the user to the dashboard so time is recorded under the correct person, and it can be revoked at any time from the dashboard’s Settings page, which immediately ends that browser’s access. Disconnecting from the extension popup also removes the token and the cached task data from that browser. Until a browser is paired, the timer does not run at all.

Third-Party Services

Data submitted by the extension is sent to the White Lotus Tasks dashboard operated by White Lotus AI. Onward processing by the firm’s own service providers (for example, cloud hosting, file storage and AI summarisation) is governed by the firm’s arrangements with those providers.

While ChatGPT chat names is on, the extension also calls ChatGPT (chatgpt.com, operated by OpenAI) under the user’s own ChatGPT account, to read the user’s chats and to change their titles. It sends ChatGPT nothing else, and nothing from ChatGPT reaches the dashboard.

What the Extension Stores in the Browser

All of the following is held locally in the browser’s extension storage and is removed when the extension is uninstalled:

  • The pairing token, dashboard address, and the paired user’s email and staff name;
  • Cached client, list and task names, refreshed roughly every ten minutes and cleared on disconnect or via Refresh in the popup;
  • Time entries that could not be sent yet, so nothing is lost when the network drops. Each carries its task, minutes, note and date, retries automatically, and is given up on after seven days rather than retried forever;
  • A local history of the most recent 100 recorded sessions (duration, note, client / list / task names, the paired email, and whether it synced), shown in the popup and clearable there with “Clear history”;
  • Parked draft timings: a one-way hash per draft plus the elapsed time, kept up to seven days so reopening a draft resumes its count. No email text is stored;
  • While markup is on, session-only state: the recording clock reported by the dashboard, whether it is paused, and any pending Pause/Stop command. This is cleared when the browser closes;
  • For ChatGPT chat names: the on/off setting and when it was switched on; cached client names and abbreviations (cleared like the other cached names); for each chat the extension renamed, the chat’s identifier, its old and new title, the client abbreviation used and when, so the rename can be undone; a short marker for chats it looked at and left as they were; and a one-line status shown in the popup. While the feature is in use, entries older than 90 days are dropped. No chat text and no ChatGPT credentials are stored.

White Lotus AI uses commercially reasonable efforts to handle information securely, but no software, browser extension, network, or third-party service can be guaranteed to be error-free, uninterrupted, or completely secure.

Limited Use Disclosure

The extension’s use of information received from Chrome APIs is limited to providing and improving the extension’s stated functionality. The extension does not use Chrome API data for advertising, sale of data, creditworthiness determinations, or unrelated profiling.

User Control

Users control when they submit time entries; nothing is logged unless a task is chosen and Save is pressed, and closing the confirmation without saving records nothing. Users choose whether to grant the optional all-sites markup permission and whether to switch on ChatGPT chat names, see every proposed name before a cleanup renames older chats and can undo a cleanup, can clear their local history, can disconnect the browser from the dashboard, and may disable or uninstall the extension through Chrome at any time. Organization administrators may also manage access, permissions, and configuration depending on how the extension is deployed.

Data Retention

The extension retains only the information reasonably necessary for its operation, troubleshooting, legal compliance, or legitimate business purposes. Local retention limits are described under “What the Extension Stores in the Browser”. Data submitted to the dashboard is retained according to the firm’s own retention practices and the settings of the applicable White Lotus Tasks workspace.

Organizational Use

If the extension is provided through an employer, client, or organization, that organization may have access to time entries, activity records, task submissions, or related work information according to its own internal policies and systems. Users should contact their organization with questions about workplace monitoring, employee records, or internal data practices.

No Guarantee of Accuracy

The extension is a productivity and time-tracking aid. It may not always capture time perfectly, detect every activity correctly, or submit information without error. Users are responsible for reviewing time entries before submitting them.

Changes to This Policy

White Lotus AI may update this Privacy Policy from time to time. Updates will be posted where this Privacy Policy is made available. Continued use of the extension after an update means the updated policy applies.
